Junior Dinghy Rating

Prerequisite: Novice Rating Unlocks: Keelboat Sailing
Unrated Novice Junior Senior Cruising Senior Advanced Dinghies

The Junior rating is the rating most active CSC sailors hold. It opens up most of the smaller boats, a bigger sailing area and keelboat lessons. Beginners usually need 20 to 30 hours of lessons and practice to get there. Experienced sailors may need less, but still need to learn CSC’s systems and procedures, such as using the hoist to launch boats.

Note: Compiled from the current Sailboat Skipper Rating and Sailing Areas pages and the Operating Rules (A.1.4, B.2, H, O.4). Please check: the Rating page says Juniors need at least 4 signed-off work hours, but the Operating Rules (H.3) say a one-time 2 hours. The previous draft of this page (no source given) also listed the UFO and the symmetric spinnaker for Juniors; the Operating Rules don’t mention either, and say Juniors can’t use bowsprits or gennakers.
Skills Required

Junior Sailing Skills Checklist

Before requesting your Junior test, be able to do all 12 of these. Your instructor can help you practice any you want more experience with.

  • 1.Know the sailing circle, and sail a steady course on each point of sail
  • 2.Sail close-hauled and close-reaching, and be confident sitting on the rail, hiking out and using the tiller extension
  • 3.Come about from close-hauled to close-hauled
  • 4.Heave to
  • 5.Gybe from a run to a run, or from a broad reach to a broad reach
  • 6.Slow sailing: crew overboard recovery and docking
  • 7.Sail backwards from the dock
  • 8.Capsize and recovery
  • 9.Anchor, especially while capsized
  • 10.Reef underway
  • 11.Sail tight circles around a buoy
  • 12.Right-of-way rules
How to Get This Rating

The Testing Process

There are three tests. You also need to complete your work hours (see Volunteer Hours). Members under 18 need ExComm approval, which may restrict teaching, Open House skippering and taking out guests until they turn 18.

1

Written Test

Covers terminology, basic skills and club rules. Taken online, with no appointment needed. It’s the same written test as for Novice.

Study guide (PDF) Take online test
2

Rigging Test

Show that you can rig and de-rig a dinghy safely and correctly on your own. Given by a Senior Sailor or a designated Junior Skipper. It’s the same rigging test as for Novice.

Skills self-assessment Request rigging test
3

On-the-Water Test

Given by a Senior Sailor. It needs at least 10 knots of wind and includes a capsize. It covers tacking, jibing, small circles, a crew overboard drill, anchoring, capsize recovery and docking. When you’re ready, fill out the skills self-assessment: answering “Yes” to the readiness question posts your request to the testers’ bulletin board. See who can sign off tests.

Skills self-assessment Request on-water test
What This Rating Unlocks

Allowed Activities

With a Junior rating, you can do everything a Novice can do, plus:

The Junior Area: within sight of the clubhouse, at least 100 yards from any downwind shore (except when heading to the dock), north of Ashby Ave and east of a line from the Emeryville Peninsula to H’s Lordships restaurant. See Sailing Areas for the full chart.
🗺️

Bigger Sailing Area

Sail in the Junior Area in winds under 20 knots, not just the Novice Area.

🕐

Sail Outside Lesson Times

Sail on your own whenever a Day Leader is on duty and a rescue skiff is working. The Day Leader may require you to reef.

🚢

Keelboat Lessons

A Junior rating is the prerequisite for keelboat lessons and all keelboat sailing.

👥

Bring Guests

Take non-member friends out sailing as your guests.

🎓

Teach Lessons

Teach dinghy lessons and earn membership credits.

⛵

More Boats

Sail RS Quests, RS Ventures, RS Touras and JY-15s (online test needed for Ventures, Touras and JY-15s). Lasers and c420s need a special checkout by a qualified member. See Advanced Dinghies.

🏁

Racing

Skipper in club races (you also need to pass the racing test).

Juniors can’t use bowsprits or gennakers. Except on a Laser, an anchor and paddle must be aboard.

Keelboat — Unrated

Keelboats are larger, more stable sailboats with enclosed cabins. Before earning a keelboat rating, you work through five skill levels under instructor supervision.

Prerequisite: Junior Dinghy Rating
Unrated Senior Cruising Senior Advanced Keelboats
Note for reviewers: Please confirm the keelboat rating policy. This page’s five level names and its rule that you must complete all five levels before testing for Senior Keelboat may have been invented for the mockup. The current site says to arrange sails with Senior Keelboat skippers on the Merits and Commanders once you’ve completed the levels. Check the levels against the Outline of Student Progression (PDF) and the Keelboat Guidelines.

How Keelboat Learning Works

Unlike dinghy sailing, keelboat learning is structured as five progressive skill levels. You work through each level with an instructor before becoming eligible to test for your Senior Keelboat rating. There is no separate "Unrated" test — the levels themselves are your pathway to the Senior rating.

Skills Required

The Five Skill Levels

Each level is signed off by a qualified instructor. You must complete all five before requesting your Senior Keelboat rating test.

L1

Dockside

Learn the parts of the keelboat, basic rigging, safety equipment, and dockside procedures. Completed before leaving the dock.

L2

Basic Sailing

Sail on all points of sail, tack, gybe, and handle the boat in open water under instructor supervision.

L3

Problems Outside

Handle unexpected situations on the water: engine failure, man overboard drills, weather changes, and emergency procedures.

L4

Sailing Close Quarters

Dock, undock, and maneuver the boat in tight spaces under power and sail. One of the most important practical skills.

L5

Intermediate Maneuvers

Spinnaker handling, anchoring, night sailing awareness, and more advanced boat handling skills.

Who we are

The Cal Sailing Club (CSC) is a nonprofit, volunteer-run sailing and windsurfing club at the Berkeley Marina, and it’s open to the public. Members take lessons and, once they earn ratings, use the club’s fleet of about 28 dinghies, 6 keelboats, and 80 windsurfing boards. Teaching sailing and windsurfing safety is the club’s main activity.

CSC is a cooperative. Membership dues and volunteer work give members unlimited lessons and use of the club’s equipment, with no extra charges for either. Volunteer members do all the teaching, maintenance and administration; the only paid position is the Day Leader, a work-study student. That’s how CSC costs far less than a commercial sailing school. It also means the boats are strong, safe and functional, but “will not win any beauty contests.”

CSC is not part of the University of California, and has been an independent nonprofit since about 1970 (see History below). The UC Aquatic Center (Cal Adventures), next door, is a separate facility run by the University.

CSC also offers free sailing to the public at its Open Houses. The California Division of Boating and Waterways funds CSC to teach boating laws, boat handling, weather and water conditions, rules of the road, equipment requirements and environmental stewardship.

History & mission

Mission: The CSC Constitution (Article II) sets out the club’s purpose: education about and participation in sailing, giving the community a way to learn sailing and safe boat handling at all skill levels, and offering outreach programs for local youth and free sailing for the public. The club is not run for profit. CSC is a 501(c)(3) charitable and educational organization and a registered California charity, and donations are welcome.

History: CSC grew out of the U.C. Yacht Club (UCYC), which once served all of UC Berkeley’s sailing needs. Around 1970, the UC Racing Team, UC sailing classes and UCYC split into three separate groups, largely because the Bay’s tough conditions made it hard for one group to balance racing, teaching and boat maintenance. UCYC became the Cal Sailing Club, an independent nonprofit, and soon moved to its current site on the South Sailing Basin of the new Berkeley Marina. In 1974, members designed and built a small clubhouse building.

Until 1979, membership was limited to UC students, faculty and alumni. That year, facing new insurance requirements and UC plans for a combined sailing program, CSC chose to stay independent, found its own insurance and officially opened membership to the public. UC’s plans became the Cal Adventures sailing program next door.

The early 1980s brought a paved and fenced boat yard, a 26-foot cruising sailboat, the first windsurfers and about 670 members by 1983. CSC became a 501(c)(3) educational organization in 1982. In 1984, loans from members paid for a new fleet of eight 14-foot boats, and by 1988 windsurfing made up about half of the club’s summer activity.

Summarized from “A History of the Cal Sailing Club” by Paul Kamen, former CSC Commodore (1995, revised 1997). No written account exists yet for the 1990s to the present.

FAQs

When is the club open?
Noon to sunset on weekdays, and 9:00 am to sunset on weekends, all year. Low tides, high winds, dense fog and thunderstorms can close the club or limit sailing, so check the Calendar for each day’s open and close times.

Can I call the club?
No. There’s no phone at the club, and we like it that way. To reach a club officer, see Contacts.

How do I join, and what does it cost?
See Join for membership types and prices. Members also contribute at least 2 hours of volunteer work every 3 months (more for ratings), such as helping at Open Houses, repairs, teaching or day leading.

How long does it take to learn?
It depends on the person, so think in hours rather than weeks. A beginner usually needs 20 to 30 hours of lessons and practice to be ready for the Junior dinghy test. The Dinghy Fast-Track Program is a quicker route.

What is the minimum age?
Children must be at least 5 to go out on a CSC boat, for example at an Open House. For dinghy lessons, children under 12 can’t take regular club lessons; ages 12 to 17 need their own membership, must take lessons with a parent or a parent-appointed adult, and need ExComm approval. CSC is an adult club and doesn’t provide supervision for children.

Do I need to be a strong swimmer or very fit?
You don’t need to be a strong swimmer, because you always wear a life jacket (PFD) on the water. You should be comfortable in the water with a PFD on, since dinghies can capsize, and be able to swim about 10 yards in a PFD. TBD: more on physical fitness.

I already know how to sail. Can I skip ahead?
Experience helps, but there’s no shortcut. Everyone gets ratings by passing CSC’s tests, and advanced ratings also depend on taking part in running the club and building trust. Every advanced sailor is expected to master dinghies. Experienced sailors often move faster because they can help right away.

What if I just want to sail keelboats?
You need a Junior dinghy rating before you can take Keelboat Lessons. See the Sailing Overview for how the path works.

Can I rent a boat or other gear?
No. CSC doesn’t rent equipment. Members use the club’s boats and boards once they have the right rating, and rated members can bring non-member friends out as guests.

Is dinghy sailing or windsurfing right for me?
See “Sailing or windsurfing?” above. TBD: more detail.

Is it safe?
CSC is a safe place to learn. Every lesson covers safety equipment and practices before you go out, and Novice and Junior sailors can only sail when a Day Leader and a working rescue skiff are on duty. For details, see the Dinghy Manual (Section 1, “Safety First”) and the Operating Rules.

Can I go on a cruise?
Yes, if you’re a member. You can bring a guest on keelboat cruises, but not on dinghy cruises. See each cruise’s details in the calendar for specifics, and see Cruising.

Can I race in YRA events?
Yes. Regular members who own their own sailboat can use CSC as their yacht club in YRA events.

What’s happening at the club right now?
See the Calendar and Club Status.

How do I cancel automatic renewal of my membership?
Auto-renew is set up in your PayPal account. Log in to PayPal, go to Payments, then Manage Automatic Payments.

Open House: Introductory Sails at the Cal Sailing Club

124 University Avenue at the Berkeley Marina (no phone: we like it that way!)

Ever wonder about those dazzling sails buzzing around on the Bay? The Cal Sailing Club monthly Open House/Introductory Sail gives you a chance to find out! Cost: zero. Fun: limitless.

The Cal Sailing Club (CSC) offers sailing to the public aboard our fleet of keelboats (large and stable) and dinghies (fast and zippy, but kinda tippy) ten times a year. Whether you are new at sailing or an “old salt,” you can enjoy a spectacular voyage within sight of the University of California, the Bay Bridge, San Francisco, and the Golden Gate!

CSC is an independent, non-profit cooperative; our popular Open Houses are a great way to get a free sail of around ½ hour in one of the most beautiful settings on the planet, and to learn about boat and waterfront safety, Bay ecology, and the fun of non-motorized watercraft.

Almost all Open Houses are held from 1-3 pm Sunday afternoons (and sometimes on Saturday to coincide with other activities at the Marina). Sign up in front of the club house between 1-2:30. See the calendar above for dates.

How can I sign up?

Rides are given on a first-come-first-served basis by volunteer CSC members. Please try to arrive at 1 pm when the signups begin at the table. On nice days we get lots of visitors. If the tide is dropping, we may have to end sailing earlier than 3 pm, though we try our best to get everyone aboard.

Is it safe?

One of the main reasons we do this is to teach the safest possible boating practices, including the most important: always wearing a personal flotation device (lifejacket) when boating. Nonetheless, there is some risk inherent in sailing, including the possibility of ending up in the water if your dinghy tips over. We require all participants to sign a waiver (click here to read the waiver for those 18 years old and older; click here to read the waiver for parents and guardians to sign on behalf of their children under 18) stating that they understand that there are risks that they are voluntarily taking on. Our skippers are trained in safe boating, and we have radios and a rescue skiff standing by in case help is needed.

Can I bring my children?

Children must be at least 5 years of age and accompanied by an adult. We recommend that first timers, small children and not-so-agile adults sail in our larger keelboats.

What should I wear?

Bring a strap for eyeglasses or sunglasses, a string to hold your cap on, shoes that can get wet (better avoid flip flops) and comfortable clothes including a windbreaker. A change of clothes can come in handy in case you get soaked. You can borrow our foulies (heavy raincoats) and a PFD (life jacket, required).

The weather looks iffy. At what point do you cancel the event?

CSC’s first priority is boating safety. If we get heavy rain or excessive winds, we may cancel. There’s always next month!

Where are you located?

CSC is just east of the Shorebird Center, at 124 University Ave. on the Berkeley Marina, facing Emeryville, about ¼ mile before the Berkeley Pier. There’s plenty of parking for cars and bikes, and AC transit stops just opposite the club. Be sure to ask the bus driver if the bus goes to the Marina.

How Sailing Works at CSC

Everyone starts with dinghy lessons in small, responsive boats. Lessons are free for members, first-come, first-served, and taught by volunteer instructors, usually one instructor and up to four students in a 14-foot dinghy.

As you learn, you earn ratings by passing a written test, a rigging test and an on-the-water test. Each rating unlocks more boats and a bigger sailing area:

  • Novice: a learner’s permit. Sail in the low-wind Novice Area under the Day Leader’s supervision, alone or with one other member.
  • Junior: the rating most active CSC sailors hold. Sail most of the smaller boats in the area between Berkeley and Emeryville, and bring guests. Beginners usually need 20 to 30 hours of lessons and practice to get there. Experienced sailors may need less, but still need to learn CSC’s systems and procedures, such as using the hoist to launch boats.
  • Senior Dinghy: for very skilled sailors who also contribute to the club. Sail any club dinghy, any time, in a much larger area.

Keelboats (larger boats with cabins) are a separate track. A Junior dinghy rating gets you into keelboat lessons, which lead to the Senior Keelboat and Cruising ratings.

San Francisco Bay is known for strong wind, big waves and cold water, so CSC’s rating tests are more demanding than most sailing programs.

Novice

🖼️Photo: Novice
Note: Compiled from the current Sailboat Skipper Rating and Sailing Areas pages and the Operating Rules (A.1.4, B.1, G, O.4).

Skills Required

The Novice sailing test covers:

  • Leaving and returning to the dock
  • Anchoring
  • Capsize recovery
  • Tacking and jibing

It can be held in any wind that moves the boat.

Process to Get This Rating

  1. Junior Skipper written test: taken online. Covers terminology, basic skills and club rules.
  2. Rigging test: given by a Senior Sailor. Some designated Junior Skippers can also give it.
  3. Novice sailing test: given by a Senior Sailor.

When you’re ready, fill out the skills self-assessment. Answering “Yes” to the readiness question posts your request to the testers’ bulletin board. See who can sign off tests.

The written and rigging tests are the same ones you need for Junior.

Allowed Activities

Novice is a learner’s permit. You can:

  • Sail in the Novice Area only: at least 100 yards from any downwind shore, north of H’s Lordships restaurant and west of the 3rd dock
  • Sail in winds under 10 knots
  • Sail alone or with one other club member
  • Sail RS Quests, RS Ventures and RS Touras (RS Ventures and Touras need an online test)
  • With the Rear Commodore’s approval, teach one student at a time in the Novice Area

You can only sail when a Day Leader is on duty and a rescue skiff is working. The Day Leader may restrict your use of boats.

Cruising Senior

🖼️Photo: Cruising Senior
Note: Compiled from the current Sailboat Skipper Rating and Sailing Areas pages and the Operating Rules (I.4, N.1, N.5). The Keelboat Cruising Senior section is from the Keelboat Rating Guidelines and the Operating Rules (I.5, N.2 to N.4). The three-option list under “Process” came from the previous draft of this page (no source given).

This page covers both cruising ratings: Dinghy Cruising Senior and Keelboat Cruising Senior (see Keelboat Cruising Senior below). The Cruising Skipper rating combines both.

Skills Required

Cruising Dinghy Skippers lead dinghies beyond the Senior Area, where wind, currents, waves and shipping traffic are much stronger. You’ll need a First Aid course and the Cruising written test. See the Cruising Dinghy Rating guidelines (PDF).

Process to Get This Rating

  1. Hold a Senior Dinghy rating
  2. Pass the Cruising written test. It’s offline: email the Cruising Chair, who emails the test back. It’s essay format, with no time limit.
  3. Complete a First Aid course
  4. Complete three qualifying dinghy cruises, each supervised by a different Cruising Skipper (see below)
  5. Get ExComm approval, based on the cruise reports

Qualifying cruises: at least 3 lottery sign-up cruises with 3 or more boats. You skipper a separate boat, stay within sight and keep radio or cell contact. At least 2 must go to one of these: Clipper Cove, north of Cesar Chavez Park, or an around-the-pier race.

  1. Dinghy Cruising Senior only: requires 3 dinghy cruises, including specific destination areas.
  2. Keelboat Cruising Senior only: requires 3 keelboat cruises, including specific destination areas.
  3. Both Dinghy and Keelboat Cruising Senior: requires 3 keelboat cruises, including specific destination areas, and one dinghy cruise.

Allowed Activities

  • Sail any club dinghy. The First Vice Commodore may require a special sign-off for some dinghies.
  • Sail in the Cruising Dinghy Area: north of a line from the Emeryville Peninsula to Clipper Cove, plus Clipper Cove itself, bounded to the east by the Treasure Island and Richmond jetty lines
  • Lead dinghy cruises, and approve Senior dinghies joining you in the Cruising Dinghy Area

Rules: all dinghies on a cruise must stay in visual contact. Single-handing outside the Senior Dinghy and Windsurfing Area needs another CSC boat. No guests on dinghy cruises.

Keelboat Cruising Senior

Lets you skipper club keelboats anywhere in San Francisco Bay and the Delta. The guidelines say the most important preparation is to log a lot of sea time.

Process:

  1. Hold a Senior Keelboat rating
  2. Pass the Cruising written test, given by the Sailing Examiner Chair. Study The Annapolis Book of Seamanship, your SF Bay chart, U.S. Chart No. 1, the Navigation Rules handbook and the Operating Rules for cruising.
  3. Complete a Standard First Aid course
  4. Complete three qualifying keelboat cruises, supervised by three different Cruising Skippers you’ve sailed with before. You do the planning and management; they observe.
  5. Get ExComm approval, based on the three recommendation letters and your first aid certificate

Qualifying cruises: you run at least 3 lottery sign-up cruises, with at least 4 different destinations from Raccoon Strait, West Bay, North Bay and South Bay. One cruise must have at least two boats, managed by you, and one must return after dark (at least half an hour after official sunset). With the supervising Cruising Skipper’s approval, you may skipper a separate boat, staying within sight and radio or phone contact.

Allowed: skipper all club keelboats in the Cruising Keelboat Area: the inland and tributary waters of San Francisco Bay, and outside the Golden Gate no farther west than a line from Point Bonita Light through Mile Rocks Light to the San Francisco shore. Cruises can’t be longer than 24 hours without advance ExComm approval. You may run one private cruise after each keelboat lottery cruise. Single-handing needs ExComm approval.

Cruising Skipper (both ratings): requires Senior Skipper, the Cruising written test, First Aid, 3 keelboat cruises and 1 dinghy cruise. Cruising Skippers run at least one lottery sign-up cruise a year.

Overview

Keelboat lessons are for members with a Junior dinghy rating or higher. The Junior rating gives you the sailing fundamentals, and keelboats work on the same principles. Lessons are usually on Wednesday evenings, and instructors sometimes offer them on other days too.

Lessons are taught on the club’s keelboats at J-Dock in the Berkeley Marina, across University Avenue from the clubhouse, next to the Marina offices. Meet at J-Dock at lesson time. Senior

🖼️Photo: Senior
Note: Compiled from the current Sailboat Skipper Rating and Keelboat Rating Guidelines pages and the Operating Rules (A.6.2, I.3, L, M). The previous draft of this page (no source given) also listed a Keelboat Senior rigging test and a grandfathering rule for written tests taken before the dinghy and keelboat tests were split. Skills Required

Senior Keelboat lets you skipper any club keelboat from Emeryville to Richmond. The practical tests are in 15+ knots of wind and cover heavy weather and usual and unusual maneuvers, chosen by the tester. See the checklist of skills tested (PDF).

You don’t need a Senior Dinghy rating first, but the guidelines say there’s no better groundwork than becoming an expert dinghy sailor.

Process to Get This Rating

  1. Hold a Junior Skipper rating, and work through the five keelboat skill levels (see Keelboat Unrated)
  2. Complete a Senior Project approved by ExComm. Pick something that takes at least 10 hours and ideally benefits other members. It can also count toward the Senior Skipper rating.
  3. Pass the Senior Keelboat written test, an online essay test graded by the Sailing Examiner Chair. You need your Junior rating and an approved project to take it, and you can only take it once.
  4. Pass two practical tests, each with a different Rating Committee member authorized for keelboats, on two different boat types
  5. Get ExComm approval, based on your judgment, sailing ability and contribution to the club

Getting ready: practice at Wednesday night lessons, weekend lessons, club cruises (ask for time at the helm) and crewing at Open House. Ask Senior Keelboat skippers to sail with you on the Merits and Commanders, and ask them to introduce you to others. When you’re ready, find testers on the Rating Committee, and be persistent.

Written test study: Colgate’s Basic Sailing, the Operating Rules, the Keelboat Manual, the Navigation Rules handbook and a San Francisco Bay chart. If you fail, the Sailing Examiner Chair will assign work to bring you up to passing level.

Allowed Activities

  • Skipper all club keelboats. The Third Vice Commodore may require a sign-off for some keelboats (see Advanced Keelboats).
  • Sail in the Senior Keelboat Area: north of a line from the southwest corner of the Emeryville Peninsula to the north end of Treasure Island, with limits to the east and south and around the Richmond Jetty and Brooks Island
  • Sign out keelboats yourself at J-Dock
  • Teach keelboat lessons and give Open House rides (the guidelines suggest only after a lot of practice)

Rules: keelboats can’t be single-handed without ExComm approval. Each keelboat must carry all Coast Guard required safety equipment, a working waterproof VHF radio and/or cell phone, an anchor and rode, paddles, a working compass, a first aid kit with basic tools, a storm jib and a bailing bucket.

Responsibilities: at least 10 hours every 3 months of teaching, repair, cleanup, day leading, Junior tests or administration, signed off on the club website.

Senior Skipper: combines Senior Dinghy and Senior Keelboat. If you already hold one, you only need the other’s sailing test.

J80 – White Sails

Senior Keelboat sailors must be signed off on the J80 to sail it. The white-sails sign-off includes flying the kite (gennaker) in low wind, 8 knots and under, so you can practice hoisting, dousing and handling it. Anyone with the full sign-off can sign you off for white sails.

What the checkout covers is up to the person signing you off. It usually includes, for both levels:

  • Hoisting, dousing and handling the kite (even for the white-sails sign-off)
  • Furling and unfurling the jib: not letting the wind unfurl it, and handling the fragile furler
  • Tensioning the jib halyard when unfurling, and loosening it before the final furl
  • Using the electric outboard
  • Putting the boat away
  • Handling high wind, including what to do when the boat won’t fall off below a beam reach
  • Why the high-aspect foils make close-quarters situations riskier

J80 – Full Sign-Off (Gennaker)

No wind limit, so you can fly the gennaker in any wind. Given by someone with the full sign-off who is also on the keelboat Rating Committee.

About Fast-Track

Fast-Track is a week of intensive evening lessons designed to teach you the skills for a Junior dinghy rating. You’ll be on the water with one other student and an instructor from 5 to 8 pm, Monday through Friday.

It’s more than sailing practice. After each evening on the water, there’s a big community dinner and a debrief with your instructor, other instructors and the other students. It’s a great way to meet the members who make CSC special.

Each year, one week is a special women/trans/non-binary week, with women/trans/non-binary students learning from women/trans/non-binary instructors.

Not sure it’s right for you? Read a blog post from a past Fast-Track student.

Fast-Track is for CSC members only. Become a member to sign up.

Prerequisites

  • Pass the rigging test

Cal Sailing Club members enjoy numerous opportunities to race club dinghies. The competition is friendly but not lax. Racing at CSC is an excellent way for new sailors to learn some of the finer points of sailing and for experienced sailors to hone their skills.

What's it like?

This is casual racing, with competitors themselves running the regatta. There is (usually) no committee boat to call starts/finishes, or make visual signals.

Competitors race one-design in the club's dinghies. We usually race JY15s, which are best sailed by two people. Other days we'll switch it up and race Quests, Ventures, Lasers, or all of the above in a mixed fleet. Boat handling and tactics reign supreme, and sometimes luck.

We usually set short courses around temporary buoys, sized for races lasting 10 to 15 minutes intended to provide more mark roundings, tactical decisions, and close encounters than would occur over a longer course. Typically there are 6 to 8 races in two hours of racing, so skippers and crew have experienced a physical and mental workout by the time it's over.

Drawings of our most common courses can be found here.

When are races?

Racing is held year round, but more frequently in the summer months. Start times and any race-specific information will usually be posted to the calendar and always sent to the announcements mailing list at least a day in advance.

Racing is rain or shine, but may be postponed or canceled due to stormy weather.

In all cases, plan to arrive at least an hour before the scheduled start time to find a partner, rig a boat, and attend the pre-race briefing.

Sunday

Racing is held nearly every Sunday of the year. The start time is traditionally 11:00 am, but can vary depending on tide and weather forecast. In the shoulder seasons we may switch to afternoons to chase the waning sea breeze.

Thursday

Thursday night racing is held from April through October starting at about 5:00pm, followed most nights by dinner.

Tuesday

Occasionally in the summertime there will be an evening "round the pier" race. Each boat must be skippered by a senior skipper, since the race leaves the bounds of the Junior Area. This race usually features boats with spinnakers to take advantage of the long downwind leg. Given the requirement for a senior skipper, these races require advanced signup.

Who can race?

All club members and their guests are welcome to race. Each boat racing must be skippered by a current member with at least a dinghy Junior rating who has passed the racing rules test online or received an exception from the race committee.

Up and coming sailors, new members with a racing background, and more: anyone looking to have fun with sailing, hone their skills, and connect with their fellow club members in a friendly environment is encouraged to attend. It's best to have at least a handful of outings on a dinghy under your belt, so consider a Saturday lesson first if you're new to sailing.

The skipper doesn't need to helm, and it's common to switch positions several times throughout the day.

Contacts for your questions

For further information about dinghy racing please contact the dinghy race chairs for Sundays or Thursdays. The #racing Slack channel is a fairly active forum for discussion of all upcoming racing and racing-adjacent events as well as tactics and techniques.

Sailing Instructions

We'll use these instructions for races unless otherwise stated.

Rules: 2025-2028 Racing Rules of Sailing.

Entry: No pre-registration needed. Every boat racing must have a responsible person (skipper) aboard who has 1. a dinghy Junior rating 2. passed the CSC racing rules test. Any member or guest may crew.

Signals: No visual signals will be made. Starting signal will be 3-minute audible signals sounded by an electronic starting horn buoy (Ollie*). In case of equipment failure, a whistle may be used. The signals are described in detail here: Start Sequences Used at CSC

Please pass on any hails made by the race committee.

Course: The course will be announced during the skippers' meeting. Most common courses are listed on this document. The course may be changed between races. Any changes will be audibly hailed. The start/finish line is open; you may sail through it when not starting/finishing.

No starting penalties unless otherwise specified during the skippers' meeting. If you start too soon, return to the pre-start side either through the line ("dip the line") or around the end.

Rule 44.1 is modified. All fouls require a one-turn penalty only. Foul someone? Take a single penalty turn with a tack and a gybe. Touch a mark? Single turn (same as the official rules). Do both at the same time? Single turn.

Scoring? Not usually. Sitting on a committee boat is no fun. Live in the moment of your victory until the next race starts.

Protests? The finer points of any rules questions arising from a day's races are discussed informally back at the dock or over Slack.

Racing Rules Test (aka Racing Skipper Written Test)

Like most sailboat racing, CSC dinghy races are conducted under the International Sailing Federation's Racing Rules of Sailing, or RRS. The current version of the RRS is 2025-2028.

All skippers racing must pass our written racing skipper test online or receive approval of the racing committee (eg. new members with previous racing experience may be granted a temporary waiver). The CSC Racing Skipper test is based strictly on the RRS circa 2010, and is open book. A pdf copy of the test is available here. You can take the test online here (you have to be logged in).

There have been updates to the RRS since CSC's handbook was published in 2010, so be sure to review the latest rules.

Overview

CSC skippers lead cruises to places around the Bay, such as the Golden Gate Bridge, Angel Island, Sausalito, Pier 39 and the Oakland Estuary. Almost all cruises are day trips, usually on the club’s keelboats, and sometimes on dinghies.

How Sign-Up Works

Only sign up if you (and your guest) are committed to going. If something comes up, remove your name as soon as possible, even if you’re on the waiting list. Last-minute cancellations and no-shows are unfair to other members and to the skippers who organize these cruises.

  • You need to be logged in, with an active membership, to sign up.
  • Give full names, and emergency contact information for yourself and your guest. Your emergency contact can’t be someone going on the cruise.
  • Popular cruises fill up, so a fair lottery decides who goes. It doesn’t matter when you sign up, as long as it’s before the lottery closes.
  • The lottery keeps you and your guest together whenever possible. A guest added after the lottery closes goes to the end of the list.
  • Canceling and signing up again won’t improve your chances: you keep your original lottery position.
  • You’ll get an email after the lottery closes telling you whether you’re on board. If you’re on the waiting list, you may still get a spot if someone cancels or a boat is added.

The skipper’s announcement gives the meeting time and place, the destination, the expected return time and what to bring. Read it carefully.

What to Bring

It can be hot, then wet and cold, on the same cruise, so be ready for both. Bring:

  • A complete set of extra clothes, plus a few extra layers to share with people who forget
  • Water and snacks
  • A little cash
  • Anything else the skipper’s announcement asks for

You’ll usually meet at the clubhouse. If you’re running late, go straight to the dock: CSC’s keelboats are at the very end of the west finger of J-Dock, and the J-Dock ramp is east of the Harbor Master’s office.

How Windsurfing Works at CSC

Everyone starts with windsurfing lessons, taught by volunteer members. Beginner lessons run on Saturday and Sunday mornings from April to October, except when tides are too low to open the club. Unlike dinghy lessons, they need sign-up in advance. No experience is needed, and you don’t need to be a strong swimmer.

As you learn, you earn ratings, which unlock bigger sailing areas, stronger wind and lighter, faster boards. Most people earn their Novice rating in their first lesson.

  • Novice: windsurf in the Novice Area in winds under 10 knots, on stable beginner boards.
  • Junior: the larger Junior Area in winds under 20 knots, on Junior boards with any sail. Juniors can teach lessons.
  • Junior+: lighter Junior+ boards in winds under 25 knots, plus the option to learn foiling.
  • Senior: the Senior Area and Senior boards, any time during daylight, even without a Day Leader.

Intermediate and advanced classes (rigging, board repair, high-wind stance, waterstarts, fast tacks, planing jibes, foiling and light-wind freestyle) are offered when advanced members volunteer to teach them, usually with about a week’s notice on the windsurfing email list.

About the Fleet

Boards are labeled by the rating needed to use them:

  • Novice (N): stable, wide trainer boards (160 to 250 liters) with centerboards
  • Junior (J): freeride boards (125 to 155 liters) with footstraps; lighter and faster, but more fragile
  • Junior+ (J+): lighter boards (90 to 120 liters) in freeride, freewave, freemove and freestyle styles
  • Foil (F): three foiling boards, including one set up for beginner lessons
  • Senior (S): the newest, most advanced carbon boards (70 to 100 liters)

Sails: lightweight Novice sails from 1.2 to 4.2 m², and Junior sails from 3.5 to 7.5 m² in wave, freestyle, freeride and slalom styles.

Gear: What You Need

The club provides everything you need to windsurf: a board, a sail, a PFD (life jacket) and a wetsuit you can borrow (or bring your own). Novices must wear a PFD. Skills Required

Novice is the first windsurfing rating. Most people earn it in the Novice I lesson. You’ll need to:

  • Know the material in the CSC Handbook for Windsurfing, for the written test
  • Rescue yourself: leave the dock with a rigged board, get the rig ready in the water, and paddle the board and rig upwind and back
  • Handle the sail

  3. Sail handling test

  • Take the Equipment Inspection clinic and the Novice II and III lessons

Skills Required

The sailing test is in 10 to 16 knots of wind, on a 160-liter board with a large sail. You need to show you can get home while overpowered. It typically covers:

  • Sailing upwind and tacking in swell
  • Sailing downwind
  • Nonplaning jibes
  • 360-degree turns using the uphaul
  • Handling waves
  • Docking

  • Teach windsurfing lessons, for lesson or work credit

Junior+

🖼️Photo: Junior+
Note: Compiled from:

Skills Required

The sailing test is in unsheltered water beyond H’s Lordships, on a board of 135 liters or less. You’ll need to:

  • Waterstart consistently on both tacks
  • Plane upwind under control on both tacks, in the harness and footstraps
  • Get back into the footstraps
  • Dock start, and dock with good technique

Carving turns help. For the rigging test, you rig and derig at least two sails, protect the sails, and recognize a properly rigged sail.

Process to Get This Rating

  1. Written test: taken online
  2. Complete sail rigging test: given by a Senior windsurfer
  3. Board repair: fix one fiberglass-damaged board (with help if needed), checked by a Senior windsurfer
  4. Sailing test: given by a Senior windsurfer
  5. Work hours: a one-time 2 hours, on top of the regular 2 hours every 3 months

Allowed Activities

  • Use Novice (N), Junior (J) and Junior+ (J+) boards, and any sail
  • Windsurf in the Junior Area (the same area as Junior) in winds under 25 knots
  • Take the foiling test (see below)

Rules: you can only windsurf when a Day Leader is on duty and a rescue skiff is working. Junior+ boards are more fragile and harder to uphaul, so build up your skills before taking them out in stronger wind. You’re responsible for damage from docking badly. Junior+ windsurfers are expected to give extra volunteer time, such as teaching and equipment upkeep.

Optional Foil White Card Privilege

Junior+ and Senior windsurfers can earn the foiling rating:

  • Test: a written test, taken online, plus a one-time 2 work hours
  • Boards: foil (F) boards, with any sail
  • Water depth: at least 4 feet on a falling tide, or 3 feet on a rising tide, measured at the end of the dock
  • Area: your Junior+ or Senior area, keeping a good buffer (more than 50 feet) from land

Expense Reimbursement

1. Get approval before making a purchase

Before spending money on club expenses, contact the ExComm member or chair or coordinator who can approve your purchase or provide a better alternative:

Category Contact
Kitchen supplies Banquet chair
Food for general club events/parties Vice Commodore, Banquet chair
Food for Fast Tracks, seminars, other teaching events Rear Commodore (sailing or windsurfing), Fast Track coordinator
Clubhouse or yard supplies, equipment, accessories Port Captain
Construction projects Commodore
Skiff maintenance supplies, gas, oil, etc. Port Captain
Dinghy parts, maintenance supplies, tools First Vice Commodore
Windsurf parts, maintenance supplies, tools Second Vice Commodore
Keelboat parts, maintenance supplies, tools Third Vice Commodore
Any of the above or other expenses Treasurer

The club has wholesale accounts or other purchasing arrangements that can be much less expensive and time-consuming than it would be for you to go to a store (even Costco). Contacting the Treasurer as well as the person(s) in charge will ensure that CSC gets the best deal and that you don't waste your time.

2. Get reimbursed

To get reimbursement for your approved purchases, email the treasurer at treasurer@cal-sailing.org and be sure to include:

  • Who approved it
  • Your PayPal, Venmo, or Zelle address for reimbursement (sorry, no checks or cash)
  • Pictures of your receipts (.jpg preferred) showing items and the total

Reimbursement usually takes a day or two, but sometimes the Treasurer falls asleep early, or something, so be ready to remind. Thanks!
